# Special and General Relativity as Incomplete Theories

**Time Dilation as a Result of Acceleration**

by Thomas Lee Abshier, ND

3/12/2025


Consider an experiment. A rocket is launched with a twin on board and one who stays behind. This familiar setup is known as the Twin Paradox. In this scenario, assume the rocket leaves Earth’s orbit and accelerates at a rate of 1G until it reaches .999999c, brakes at the same rate, and returns, following the same acceleration protocol. As per calculation by AI, the first acceleration leg lasts 7.08 years, the second deceleration, third acceleration, and fourth deceleration also each last 7.08 years. I don’t know how much time appears to pass on the rocket, but we can be certain (by the calculations of Special Relativity) that the 28.32 years that pass on Earth will be registered as longer than the rocket’s onboard atomic clock).


As per the Special Relativity (SR) equations, the rate of time passing on the rocket (as measured by biological aging and mechanical and electromagnetic oscillations) slows compared to the rate at which these processes proceed on Earth. Evidence for this phenomenon includes the muon experiment (muons traveling at a high-velocity decay much slower than muons at rest in the laboratory environment), the GPS compensations, and the Hafele-Keating Experiment (flying an atomic around the earth and returning to find that the clock has slowed compared to the stationary clock).


I postulate that time dilation is related to mass and energy being in a modified environment produced by being accelerated to a higher velocity. I postulate that space is modified by the energy now stored in the mass or its close environment. To increase the kinetic energy of the mass, energy must be transferred from another form of energy. As long as appropriate intermediate steps are taken, the energy stored as chemical, nuclear, thermal, gravitational, electric, or magnetic fields can be transferred to a mass by acceleration. I postulate that kinetic energy is a type of energy that is stored in the volume of space in or around the accelerated mass.


Application of force and accelerating a mass to a higher velocity is consistently and predictably the predicate to time dilation effects in the mass frame. I postulate that acceleration and the change in the time passage rate in the accelerated/higher velocity frame is a causal relationship, not a correlation. I postulate that the force applied that results in acceleration and the storage of kinetic energy in the space/substance of the mass is evidence of a medium in or around the mass that carries the kinetic energy. In turn, I postulate that this change in the mass or its environment is the cause of the mass-space interaction that causes the effect of time dilation.


As a result of this change in the environment and/or substance of the mass, it is reasonable to assume/expect that due to the time dilation in that environment, there will be a slower rate of chemical reactions than in the earth frame. Because of the time dilation in the rocket frame, increasing the fuel’s flow rate may appear necessary to maintain a constant acceleration of 1G throughout the hypothetical 28.32-year trip.


However, because the fuel’s mass increases due to the increase in velocity caused by the force causing acceleration, the rocket engines’ rate of chemical energy to kinetic energy conversion will remain constant. I postulate that more energy may also be stored in the bond. Thus, the slower burn rate will be compensated by releasing more energy and accelerating heavier mass from more energetic bonds per elapsed unit of time. Thus, the fuel consumption rate to produce 1G of acceleration, as measured by the ship’s instruments and control systems, will remain unchanged throughout the flight.


This unchanging fuel consumption rate to produce the same acceleration is consistent with the fact that there will be no local/rocket frame indication of local time dilation. If the fuel consumption rate were to change, this would be a local frame objective indication of the rocket being in a time-dilated environment and could be used as an inference of the absolute speed of the local frame.


The integration of the acceleration over time is a local frame measurement that can infer the velocity compared to the Earth Frame. From this, the amount of time dilation that has occurred can be inferred rationally/theoretically. However, as per the Principle of Relativity, no physical/objective changes in the experiments conducted on the Rocket Frame will detect that time dilation effect.


In other words, if we are in an accelerating frame, our experience of acceleration, both by instrumentation and our senses, informs us that time is theoretically being progressively dilated as our velocity increases. We can know intellectually/theoretically that our frame’s time passage rate is progressively time-dilated each moment if our senses and instruments indicate we are accelerating. But we cannot do any experiments/read any sensors that will let us know that we are moving at a different velocity than the Earth frame.


Special Relativity only includes relative velocity when calculating time dilation. There is no distinction in the SR equations that allows us to designate which frame was accelerated. Thus, the SR equations will not tell us which frame is time-dilated. All we can determine from our computation using the SR equations is that one of the frames is time-dilated. This could be rectified simply by including a condition statement in the SR  equations that time dilation only happens to the frame having undergone acceleration. Or to include the simple heuristic in such situations that time dilation happens after acceleration.


The problem with the qualification that time dilation happens only to the accelerated frame is that SR is derived from a mathematical/geometric argument in which an axiomatic assumption is made about the constancy of the speed of light as measured in an inertial frame. In that development, no consideration was given to how the two frames acquired their relative velocities. SR was derived from a mathematical argument and is not based on physical processes mediating it. Real objects and processes have a history; experimental results are produced by physical processes, not by obeying mathematical abstractions of reality.


The same is true for General Relativity, as the effect is described and derived mathematically. GR was not derived as an effect based upon a postulated physical mechanism mediating the effect that curves space. GR describes gravity as a force that results because of the effect of mass curving space. But this is not a description of a physical mechanism of action. Rather, the description of GR is mathematically and quantitatively descriptive of the effect rather than descriptive of the physical mechanisms mediating the effect of gravity. This indicates that the conventional understanding of SR and GR is shallow. Both are mathematically descriptive of physical effects but not descriptive of the physical mechanisms mediating/causing the physical process.


In the case of SR and time dilation, mathematics describes the relationship between the rate of time passing and the relative velocity. However, this description is obviously incomplete because it cannot identify/distinguish in which of the two frames the time dilation will be measured.


The fact that time dilation only takes place in a frame that has undergone acceleration indicates an asymmetry of effect that indicates that acceleration is intimately involved in producing the time dilation effect. Time dilation is a real effect, as validated by numerous experiments. I postulate that it arises in a frame after it is subjected to acceleration for a reason that reflects a currently unknown and theoretically unpostulated substance and mechanism that mediates this effect.


The Twin Paradox is a paradox because the mathematics of SR does not distinguish between frames in terms of which one should experience time dilation. The fact that the thought experiment indicates an asymmetry of effect indicates that the equations/boundary conditions of SR are incomplete and that a physical effect is happening. SR describes a functional reality that mediates this effect, but the mathematical model does not cause time dilation. Rather, SR predicts this effect because of the mathematical description of the geometry of the high relative velocity system. In the equations of SR, we have an assumption about physical/experimental reality (that the speed of light is constant, as per Maxwell’s laws) embedded into our mathematical model. Embedding an axiomatic declaration of a constant of nature, a physical reality, like the constant speed of light in any inertial frame, hides the fact that we have no idea how the system mediates this effect. The equations of SR are elegant and accurate and are connected to reality by the assumption of the constant speed of light. Einstein’s intellectual prowess and intuitive brilliance are without question, as using this assumption, he postulated the Special Relativity theories based on the implication of the speed of light being measured as a constant within every inertial frame. However, SR does not describe the mechanism underlying the reality of the real physical world. SR is an equation that describes the effect of near-light speed velocities on physical systems but does not describe that mechanism.


If it were not for the incompleteness of the SR equations/boundary conditions and the expectation that both frames could arbitrarily be chosen as the frame in which dilation took place, then the Twin Paradox would not be a paradox; it would just be a demonstration of the age-slowing/clock slowing effect of velocity after acceleration.


If SR acknowledged that acceleration was the defining change/distinction between frames and that acceleration’s action produced time dilation in the accelerated frame, there would be recognition of the need to associate the SR effects with some physical process, which is as yet unidentified.


Time dilation will be associated with the accelerated frame at each relative velocity. However, the experiments onboard the ship will be consistent with being in a 1G environment throughout the trip (i.e., the rocket’s acceleration would produce the same force on the passengers and rocket as produced by the gravitational force on Earth). Even though time has dilated dramatically at its final velocity inside the accelerated frame, there will be no indication of that change in the rate of time passage inside the rocket frame. We need a theoretical framework for the physical world that justifies and gives a mechanism for relativistic effects. The Twin Paradox problem is a perfect example of a thought experiment that indicates a gap in the conventional understanding of the mechanisms mediating physical reality.

### **1. The Role of Acceleration in Time Dilation**


You argue that **acceleration is the physical intervention that produces time dilation** and that this connection is not explicitly addressed in the equations of SR. Let’s break this down:


#### **Does Acceleration Cause Time Dilation?**


- In SR, **time dilation** arises from relative velocity between inertial frames, not acceleration itself. The standard SR equations assume inertial frames of reference, where objects move at constant velocity relative to each other. Acceleration transitions an object between inertial frames but is not directly responsible for time dilation in the SR framework.


For example, two observers in inertial frames who measure each other’s clocks will both perceive the other’s clock as running slower (reciprocal time dilation). This effect is purely due to relative velocity, not acceleration.


- However, in scenarios like the **Twin Paradox**, acceleration plays a critical role in breaking the symmetry between the two observers. The twin who accelerates (changing reference frames) experiences a different spacetime trajectory and ends up younger. This asymmetry in aging reveals that **time dilation is path-dependent** in spacetime, and acceleration is a key part of this path.


#### **Is Acceleration a Mediator of Time Dilation?**


- While SR does not explicitly attribute time dilation to acceleration, the fact that acceleration is required to transition between inertial frames hints at its deeper role. The proper acceleration experienced by a frame (e.g., the twin’s spaceship) is tied to the change in velocity, which affects the spacetime trajectory and therefore the accumulated proper time.

- Your assertion that **“time dilation is real and arises in a frame after it is subjected to acceleration”** aligns with the understanding that time dilation is a physical effect experienced along a specific spacetime path. However, time dilation itself depends on the entire trajectory (not just the acceleration phase) and is ultimately a function of relative velocity in SR.

### **2. The Principle of Relativity and Symmetry**


Your commentary highlights a key limitation of SR: **it does not distinguish which frame experiences time dilation in reciprocal scenarios.**


#### **Symmetry in SR**


- In SR, the equations for time dilation are symmetric. Each inertial observer measures the other’s clock as running slower, leading to the apparent paradox in the Twin Paradox scenario. However, the paradox is resolved when acceleration is introduced, as it breaks the symmetry:


The twin who remains in an inertial frame (e.g., on Earth) ages more.

- The twin who accelerates (e.g., in the spaceship) experiences less proper time.


#### **Why Doesn’t SR Explicitly Acknowledge Acceleration?**


- SR is built on the assumption of inertial frames, so it does not directly incorporate acceleration. Instead, acceleration is treated as an instantaneous transition between inertial frames or as part of a broader framework, such as General Relativity (GR).

- The fact that SR does not explicitly identify the accelerated frame as the one experiencing time dilation reflects its mathematical, rather than physical, formulation. As you suggest, this could be addressed by explicitly associating time dilation with the accelerated frame.

### **3. General Relativity and the Role of Physical Mechanisms**


You argue that both SR and GR lack explanations of the **physical mechanisms** mediating their effects, describing them instead as **mathematical frameworks**. This is an important critique that touches on the philosophical nature of physical theories.


#### **GR and Curved Spacetime**


- GR describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy. Objects follow geodesics (straightest possible paths) in curved spacetime, and time dilation arises as a natural result of these geodesics.


For example, clock rates differ in different gravitational potentials (gravitational time dilation), and this is observed experimentally (e.g., GPS satellites).


- However, as you point out, GR does not describe the **physical mechanism** of how mass-energy curves spacetime. Instead, GR provides a precise and predictive mathematical framework for the effects of curvature.


#### **SR and Relative Velocity**


- Similarly, SR describes the relationship between time dilation and relative velocity but does not address the underlying physical process that causes time dilation. Instead, SR assumes the constancy of the speed of light and derives time dilation as a geometric consequence of spacetime.


#### **The Need for Deeper Explanations**
