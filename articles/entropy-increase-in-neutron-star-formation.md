The process of gravitational collapse, such as the transition from a white dwarf to a neutron star or during a supernova, is complex and involves both **entropy considerations** and the interplay of energy transfer mechanisms to satisfy the laws of thermodynamics. ### **1. The Transition: Electrons + Protons → Neutrons**


#### **a. Fewer Particles, Lower Entropy?**


- During the collapse of a white dwarf, electrons and protons combine via the reaction:





e−+p→n+νe,e^- + p \to n + \nu_e,e−+p→n+νe​,


producing neutrons and emitting neutrinos. This process is known as **electron capture**.

- At first glance, this reduces the number of free particles (electrons and protons merging into neutrons), which might suggest a **decrease** in entropy.


However, entropy is not merely about the number of particles—it’s about the total number of **accessible microstates**. Several factors ensure entropy is **maintained or increased** during this process:


---


### **2. Mechanisms That Maintain or Increase Entropy**


#### **a. Neutrino Emission**


- Neutrinos are produced in vast quantities during electron capture and escape the collapsing core. These neutrinos are extremely energetic and carry away a significant amount of entropy.

- Even though the core itself might lose some entropy due to fewer free particles, the overall system (core + neutrinos) sees an **increase in entropy**. Neutrino emission is a key factor in satisfying the **Second Law of Thermodynamics**.


#### **b. Kinetic Energy of Neutrons**


- The collapse increases the **density** of the core, compressing matter into a much smaller volume. This leads to:


Higher **Fermi momentum** of the neutrons (since neutrons now obey the Pauli exclusion principle as fermions in a degenerate state).

- Increased **kinetic energy** of the neutrons.


- The increase in kinetic energy contributes to the entropy of the neutron star, as the neutrons occupy a larger phase space in momentum.


#### **c. Gravitational Energy and Heat**


- During the collapse, gravitational potential energy is released as the core shrinks. This energy is converted into:


**Internal heat**: The temperature of the collapsing core rises, increasing thermal entropy.

- **Shock waves**: If the collapse halts abruptly (e.g., due to neutron degeneracy pressure), a rebound shock forms, triggering a **supernova explosion** (see below).


- The heat generated by compression adds to the total entropy of the system.


---


### **3. Role of the Supernova Explosion**


#### **a. Rebound Shock and Ejected Material**


- When the core collapses and the density becomes high enough to be supported by **neutron degeneracy pressure**, the infall halts abruptly. This sudden stop generates a **rebound shock wave** that propagates outward.

- The shock wave heats the outer layers of the star, ejecting them in a supernova explosion. The ejected material is highly energetic and turbulent, with a large number of accessible microstates, which significantly increases the total entropy of the system.


#### **b. Neutrino Heating**


- Neutrinos emitted during the collapse also play a role in heating the outer layers of the star, contributing to the entropy of the supernova explosion.


---


### **4. Entropy Evolution in Gravitational Collapse**


#### **a. Local Entropy Decrease in the Core**


- In the collapsing core, entropy per baryon might temporarily decrease because:


The number of free particles decreases as electrons and protons combine into neutrons.

- The matter becomes more ordered due to the formation of a neutron degenerate state.


#### **b. Total Entropy Increase**


- The **Second Law of Thermodynamics** is satisfied because the total entropy of the system (including neutrinos, ejected material, and the environment) increases dramatically:


Neutrino emission accounts for a significant entropy increase.

- The supernova explosion spreads energy and matter into a much larger volume, greatly increasing the number of accessible states.


#### **c. Neutron Star Entropy**


- The newly formed neutron star is a degenerate system of neutrons, with entropy dominated by its thermal state and the phase space of degenerate neutrons.

- The neutron star itself may have lower entropy per baryon compared to the progenitor white dwarf, but this is offset by the entropy carried away by neutrinos and the explosion.


---


### **5. The Role of Kinetic Energy**


While the kinetic energy of neutrons increases during compression, the **entropy increase** is not solely due to this factor:


- Kinetic energy contributes to the **thermal entropy** of the neutron star.

- However, the largest contributions to entropy come from:


Neutrino production and escape.

- The chaotic, high-energy supernova explosion.


---


### **6. Summary**


The transition from a white dwarf to a neutron star involves complex processes, but the **Second Law of Thermodynamics** is upheld through mechanisms that increase the total entropy of the system. Specifically:


- **Neutrino emission** carries away energy and entropy.

- **Gravitational collapse** releases energy, increasing the thermal entropy of the core and ejecta.

- The **supernova explosion** spreads matter and energy into a much larger volume, significantly increasing entropy.


While the core itself may experience a temporary **local decrease in entropy** due to particle fusion and ordering into a degenerate state, the overall entropy of the system (core + neutrinos + ejecta) **increases**, satisfying the Second Law.
